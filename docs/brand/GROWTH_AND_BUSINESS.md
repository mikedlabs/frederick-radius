# Frederick Radius growth and business plan

Working plan — July 22, 2026

Frederick Radius should earn attention by being useful at the moment someone
needs it. It should earn business revenue by saving owners time, keeping their
information current, and showing the actions Radius sent them. Payment must
never decide which restaurant is nearest, which event appears first, or what
Ask Radius recommends.

## The position

Radius is not another paid directory. Google lets a business manage its basic
profile on Search and Maps at no charge. Visit Frederick currently charges $50
per year for membership that includes a customized web listing and other
tourism benefits. Downtown Frederick Partnership, Visit Frederick, and the
Frederick County Chamber already publish events, directories, newsletters, and
marketing opportunities.

The opening for Radius is different:

- Help a person act on current local information without checking several
  feeds.
- Give an owner one quick place to correct facts and publish a time-sensitive
  update.
- Connect a listing, an event, a map result, an NFC card, and a useful action
  without changing organic ranking.
- Serve the rest of Frederick County as deliberately as downtown Frederick.

Do not describe Radius as the official source, the only guide, or a replacement
for existing local organizations. It can link their information to the moment
when it is useful.

## The trust rule

Organic discovery and paid work must be separate systems.

- Nearby, open-now, search, map, Today, and Ask Radius results rank by the
  published relevance rules. A payment cannot add a ranking point.
- A business can correct its name, address, hours, phone, accessibility facts,
  closure status, and official links without paying.
- Paid placements say `Sponsored by [business]` where the user sees the
  message. Do not hide the disclosure on an About page or after a “more” tap.
- A sponsor may buy a clearly bounded campaign position. It cannot buy the
  first answer to a user’s question.
- Radius does not sell individual location history, searches, saved places, or
  audience lists.
- Published performance reports use aggregate counts and state what each count
  means.

This is both a product promise and a sales advantage. A business is paying for
tools and work, not a secret advantage over its neighbors.

## How users find Radius

### Useful deep links

Every event, place, live update, map view, and Ask Radius answer needs a stable
link with a useful preview. Sharing should open the exact item, not the home
page. Measure whether the visitor then calls, opens directions, visits the
official site, saves the item, or adds the event to a calendar.

### Countertop NFC and QR

Give a charter business one small card that opens its live Radius page. The
page can show today’s hours, current updates, directions, and nearby plans. The
printed URL stays visible beside the QR code. Each card gets its own campaign
code so Radius can distinguish a tap from a scan or an ordinary visit.

The card is useful to the business only if the destination stays current. Do
not print a card that opens a generic promotion page or requires an account.

### Event sharing

After an organizer submits or updates an event, give them a clean share card
and a deep link. The event page should carry source, freshness, parking or
transit information when available, weather or safety context when relevant,
and nearby places without burying the event details.

### Original Frederick photography

Use owned photographs to explain a place, route, town, or change. A post should
name what is in the image and link to a useful Radius view. Avoid generic photo
dumps whose only instruction is to “explore.”

### Corrections and field mapping

Make it easy to report a closed business, wrong hour, missing trash can, moved
bench, inaccessible entrance, or stale event. Tell the contributor when the
change is reviewed. A monthly public note can show what changed without
publishing private contributor information.

### Local relationships

Use Visit Frederick, Downtown Frederick Partnership, the Chamber, municipal
Main Street organizations, event organizers, libraries, and business groups as
places to listen and distribute accurate links. Ask before using a logo or
describing an organization as a partner. A submitted event or linked public
source is not an endorsement.

### Facebook without feed filler

Run a Frederick Radius Page as a service desk, not a quote account. Meta Pages
support action buttons and show Page-level reach and action data. Post when
there is a current answer, a corrected local fact, a useful map, a field note,
or an owned photograph with context. Reply to specific corrections in public
and move personal information to a private channel.

Do not pay to boost a post until its link already produces useful actions from
organic visitors. When testing paid reach, use a small fixed lifetime budget,
one geographic audience, and one action goal. Meta states that ad cost varies;
do not build a forecast around a promised cost per click.

## What to measure

The main number is **weekly useful actions from people in or planning for
Frederick County**. A useful action is a directions open, phone call, official
website or menu visit, reservation handoff, calendar add, save, follow, or
completed correction. Keep the categories separate in reporting.

| Measure | Why it matters | First 90-day use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Useful-action rate | Shows whether a visit led somewhere practical | Compare by landing page and acquisition source |
| Returning visitors at 7 and 30 days | Shows whether Radius became a habit | Compare Today, Map, Events, and Ask Radius cohorts |
| Zero-result rate | Finds holes such as “coffee in Urbana” | Track by town, category, time, and query |
| Stale or disputed result rate | Protects trust | Count corrections per 1,000 result views |
| Correction response time | Shows whether the guide can stay reliable | Target a human response within two business days during beta |
| Business update action rate | Tests owner value | Actions within seven days of a published update |
| NFC/QR activation | Tests the physical loop | Unique card opens and useful actions, not raw scans alone |
| Cost per activated local user | Keeps promotion honest | Include design, print, ad spend, and operator time |

Do not use pageviews, follower count, or impressions as proof of business value
without the action number beside them.

## First 90 days

### Days 1–14: establish the baseline

- Confirm analytics for Today, Map, Events, Ask Radius, place details, outbound
  actions, deep-link source codes, corrections, and NFC/QR codes.
- Record the current zero-result and stale-result rates by town.
- Write the public ranking and sponsorship policy before taking money.
- Make the business claim flow honest about what is live and what is still a
  pilot.
- Do not buy ads yet.

### Days 15–30: learn from owners and users

- Interview 8–12 businesses across downtown, another municipality, and the
  northern or western county. Include a restaurant, retailer, brewery or
  winery, venue, service business, and seasonal operator.
- Ask what they update repeatedly, where customers get wrong information, what
  they paid to promote in the last year, and what result made that spend worth
  it.
- Watch at least ten local users complete a current task on their own phones.
- Recruit a small charter group only after the problem is specific.

### Days 31–60: run a concierge pilot

- Give each charter business a claimed page, trackable link, and NFC/QR card.
- Let owners submit current details and one-off updates. Review them by hand so
  the real moderation cost is visible.
- Send a short weekly report with calls, direction opens, official-site visits,
  saves, and update performance. Do not estimate store visits.
- Publish corrections and known coverage gaps on the trust page.
- Test one Facebook post format at a time. Keep the useful action as the goal.

### Days 61–90: ask for payment

- Offer the paid pilot to businesses that used the tools, not to the whole
  county at once.
- Ask for an actual card payment or signed project scope. Praise is not pricing
  validation.
- Keep the price only if owners renew and the service time leaves a workable
  margin.
- Publish one plain monthly Radius note: coverage added, important corrections,
  product reliability, and sponsored work.

The working validation gate is four paying renewals from an eight-business
charter group after a no-cost pilot. This is an internal target, not a market
benchmark. If the group will not pay, change the offer before changing the
sales copy.

## Business offer

### Radius Listing — free

- Accurate base listing and corrections.
- Owner claim and ownership review.
- Hours, contact details, accessibility facts, official menu or booking links,
  closure status, and source notes.
- Reasonable-use event and special submissions with moderation.
- Basic aggregate outbound-action count.

Accuracy is part of the public guide. It is not an upgrade.

### Radius Signal — paid tool pilot

Working test range: **$19–$39 per location each month** after a no-cost charter
period.

- Schedule an event, special, sold-out notice, or temporary-hours change.
- Manage the destination behind a Radius NFC/QR card without reprinting it.
- See aggregate calls, directions, official-link visits, saves, and follows.
- Use tagged share links and download a simple monthly report.
- Send a limited follower update after moderation.

Start at the low end only if an account needs less than 15 minutes of monthly
support. Charge per location when each location has separate hours, updates, or
reporting. Do not promise reach; report the audience and actions that actually
happened.

### Radius Studio — scoped service

Working test range: **$250–$750 per project**, or a separately scoped monthly
retainer when the workload is known.

Possible work includes a local photo session, photo-library cleanup, a campaign
page, an NFC/QR print set, event packaging, listing consistency review, or a
measured sponsored story. Quote the hours, deliverables, rights, printing, and
revision limit. A Studio client gets better production work, not better organic
ranking.

Do not launch a broad “premium listing” tier. Local organizations and Google
already provide listing products cheaply or free. Radius Signal must prove
that current updates and attributable actions solve a different problem.

### Who to recruit first

Start with businesses whose information changes often enough to make the tool
useful. Event venues and breweries need current schedules. Restaurants change
specials, service windows, and holiday hours. Seasonal attractions can close
for weather or capacity. Multi-location operators need separate facts and
reporting for each place.

Do not start with a business that rarely changes, has little direct consumer
traffic, or only wants a higher rank. It is unlikely to get enough value from
Radius Signal, and its support time will hide whether the product works.

## Validate before setting final prices

1. Ask about the owner’s last real promotion or information problem. Do not ask
   only, “Would you pay for this?”
2. Run the task manually and count the minutes spent on setup, moderation,
   support, reporting, design, and corrections.
3. Show a sample report using the business’s own pilot data.
4. Present one monthly tool price and one scoped service price.
5. Ask for payment, then watch renewal and usage for two billing cycles.

The minimum price must cover operator time, usage-based services, payment fees,
support, and a reserve for corrections or refunds. Stripe’s current standard
U.S. online-card price is 2.9% plus 30 cents per successful domestic-card
transaction. Verify that rate again before launch. A $29 subscription that
needs an hour of manual work each month is a loss even before hosting or API
costs.

## Costs and risks

| Cost or risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Maps, place data, AI, email, push, image, and hosting usage | Measure cost by feature and set request limits before promotion |
| Manual verification and moderation | Record handling time during the pilot; create rules before automating |
| Photography, design, printing, and NFC fulfillment | Quote them as scoped work or pass-through costs, not an unlimited subscription benefit |
| Wrong hours or misleading live claims | Show source and freshness; make corrections free and fast |
| Sponsor pressure on recommendations | Keep paid inventory outside organic ranking and publish the rule |
| Privacy exposure | Collect the minimum owner and user data; report aggregates; set retention periods |
| Alcohol-related campaigns | Review age, platform, and Maryland requirements before accepting the work |
| Government or organizational confusion | Use the independent-project disclosure and get written permission before using partner language or logos |
| One-person support load | Limit the charter group, office hours, post volume, and revision count |

Budget paid acquisition only after the organic and physical loops work. Track
the fully loaded cost, including Mike’s time. A cheap ad that brings visitors
who take no useful action is still expensive.

## How to pitch a business

Use a specific observation, show the working page, and explain the trust rule.

> Radius already includes Frederick County businesses for free. I noticed your
> [Sunday hours / event link / menu] is hard to confirm across the places people
> check. I am testing an owner tool that keeps that information current, gives
> you a trackable Radius link and NFC card, and reports calls, direction opens,
> and official-site visits. Paying does not move you up in search. I would like
> to run it with you for [period], then show you the real results before you
> decide whether to keep it.

For a service project:

> I can also handle the photo, campaign page, card design, and measurement as a
> fixed project. The scope is [deliverables] for [price]. The sponsored work is
> labeled, and it does not change Radius recommendations.

Do not open with the technology stack, the number of APIs, or the hours spent
building the app. Those facts can explain the work after the owner understands
the problem it solves.

## What Radius must not sell

- A higher organic rank, preferred Ask Radius answer, or “best” label.
- A fee to correct a factual error or mark a closure.
- Fake reviews, paid popularity, undisclosed endorsements, or inflated reach.
- A “verified” badge based only on payment.
- Individual user searches, exact location trails, saved items, or contact
  lists.
- Category exclusivity that hides competing businesses.
- Emergency, air-quality, road, transit, or civic information as sponsored
  advice.
- Government-looking seals, partner language without permission, or an
  implication that a linked source endorses Radius.
- Unlimited manual marketing inside a low-price software subscription.

## Copy rules

### Public posts: do

- Name the place, date, time, source, and useful action when they matter.
- Use complete sentences and ordinary words.
- Say what changed: “Cafe Nola’s Sunday hours were corrected today from the
  business’s site.”
- Put `Sponsored by [name]` at the beginning of paid copy and on the visual.
- State a limitation directly: “The county feed has not updated since 8:10
  a.m.; check the linked official alert before traveling.”
- Ask for a precise correction or task someone tried to complete.

### Public posts: do not

- Write “Discover Frederick’s hidden gems” or another tourism-magazine filler
  line.
- Stack fragments such as “Local. Live. Yours.”
- Call Radius revolutionary, game-changing, seamless, ultimate, or powered by
  “cutting-edge AI.”
- Claim “everyone in Frederick” or “thousands of locals” without current
  measured evidence.
- Treat a linked source, submitted event, or paid campaign as a partnership.
- Put a sponsor disclosure after the fold, in comments, or only on the profile.

Good:

> Alive @ Five starts at 5 p.m. Radius has the official event link, gate
> location, weather context, and nearby places open afterward. Event details
> are from Downtown Frederick Partnership.

Bad:

> Your perfect Frederick night starts here. Discover more. Live local.

Paid:

> Sponsored by [business]. [Business] paid for this post. Payment did not
> affect Radius search or recommendations.

### Business outreach: do

- Mention one fact checked on the owner’s current Radius page.
- Show the exact owner tool or deliverable.
- State the pilot period, price, moderation, and measurement in writing.
- Say that payment does not affect rank.
- Follow up with the result or correction, not a generic “bumping this.”

### Business outreach: do not

- Send a fake-personalized mass note.
- Use “exclusive opportunity,” “guaranteed exposure,” or “dominate local
  search.”
- Promise foot traffic Radius cannot measure.
- Hide printing, photography, ad spend, or revision costs in a vague package.
- Ask for account passwords. Use delegated access or owner-supplied material.

## Decision gates

Do not turn on self-serve business payments until the claim flow, moderation,
correction path, privacy language, billing cancellation, and sponsor labeling
have been tested on mobile.

Do not sell sponsored inventory until Radius can show its real audience by
town, the exact placement, the disclosure, and the action being measured.

Do not expand past the charter group until owner support can be handled inside
the promised response time without slowing corrections for the public guide.
